Bus Traffic Fever Level 1 Guide: The First Gear

There's no trick to Level 1 — it's all timing. Copy the video's first pull, let the lane open on its own, and resist tapping a second bus before the first one settles.

The First Gear in Level 1

A nearly empty lot with one or two buses and a single passenger group. Nothing to untangle here — the board is just checking that you can match the opening rhythm instead of tapping on reflex.

Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Match the first slide to the video and stop. If the opening pull is right, the level's already won before you make a second move.

First Moves: Match the Opening Slide

  1. Watch the video's first slide once, then pause.
  2. Copy that exact pull — slide the nearest bus toward the open lane.
  3. Wait for the passenger group to clear before you touch a second bus.

Midgame: Repeating the Rhythm

  1. With the board settled, run the opening once more.
  2. Match your pull speed to the video's pace.
  3. Treat the repeat as practice, not a fresh attempt.

Finishing: Lock It In

  1. Check the passenger group is gone and the lane has opened.
  2. Move the next bus only after the first lane settles.
  3. Replay the opening once more so the timing sticks.

Bus Traffic Fever Level 1 FAQ

How do I beat Bus Traffic Fever Level 1?+

Watch the walkthrough and copy the first slide exactly. It's a timing tutorial, so matching that opening pull matters more than anything else on the board.

Do I need to watch the whole Bus Traffic Fever Level 1 video?+

No — just the opening. The first slide is the whole lesson, so the first few seconds are enough to clear the level.

Why is Bus Traffic Fever Level 1 important if it is so easy?+

It locks in the slide-and-collect rhythm every later level reuses. Skip it and the harder boards feel off-tempo.

Do I need to unlock anything before Bus Traffic Fever Level 1?+

No, Level 1 is open from the start. If it isn't showing, your app might be a limited demo, so check the store listing.

Is Bus Traffic Fever Level 1 just a tutorial?+

Pretty much. It introduces the loop with almost no traffic — think of it as a timing warm-up, not a puzzle.
